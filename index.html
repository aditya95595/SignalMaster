HOW TO PLAY

Your job

You are the signaller on a real panel interlocking board. Trains move live on the board as black headcode berths on the track line. Set each route entry to exit: click a round manual signal as the entry, then click a highlighted exit — the next automatic signal, the next manual signal, or the boundary arrow. Junction points swing to Reverse by themselves when you pick a branch exit; loop and crossover points you work by hand by clicking them, and points are also worked by hand for a Pass at danger move during a failure. If the panel refuses, the reason is shown in the hint bar and the event log. Absolute Block rules apply: 1 train per block, 1 train per platform.

Controlled signals (round lamps) are absolute signals at stations, junctions, loops and crossovers. Routes are set entry to exit: click a round controlled signal to pick the entry, then click a highlighted exit — the next automatic signal, the next controlled signal downstream, or a boundary arrow / buffer stop at the panel edge. Points swing automatically and the sections between the two signals turn bold white when that signal is Clear (green). Click an entry signal that already has a route for the route popup and Cancel route. Call-on is OFF by default and is turned on from the entry panel before you pick the exit.

Automatic Block Signals (square lamps, marked AUTO) sit in the open section between stations. They work themselves, Green/Clear when the block ahead is free, Red automatically when it is occupied, and no route starts from one. Clicking one shows: this is an automatic signal, routes start from controlled signals only.

Weather changes the railroad: Rain = wet rails, trains brake a little earlier. Leaf fall = low adhesion, trains brake much earlier and track circuits fail more often. Snow = frozen switches and a speed limit, trains brake earlier. Heat = speed limits and more track-circuit failures. Weather for your shift is under the clock in the top bar.

Modes: Signal counts toward stars, unlocks and career points. Practice is your own private copy, pause or change speed any time, no unlock progress. Watch is view-only with an auto-signaller running the panel, no progress earned, cheer reactions are just for fun. The Tutorial Training Box walks one train in and out, step by step.

SUPPORT

In-game help

Stuck on a map? Open How to Play from the Main Menu for the full aspect, dwell and scoring tables. During a shift, the yellow hint bar under the board always tells you the next thing that needs your attention, and every refusal (locked point, occupied block) is explained there and in the event log.

Common problems

• Signal will not clear: the block ahead is occupied, or you set it to Manual Red. Check the block lights on the board.
• Point will not move: a train is on the point or approaching it. Wait for the lock light to turn green.
• Train held at a platform: dwell is still running, or the controlled signal ahead is not Green.
• Deadlock warning (Map 2): two opposing trains face each other on the single line. Route one into the loop with PT-PLP-01 / PT-PLP-02.
• Sound: use the Mute button on the Main Menu or in the game top bar.

HOW TO SIGNAL

Your job

You are the signaller. Trains arrive at the edges of your panel and follow the timetable. Set routes so every train reaches the right platform and leaves on time. You only score for delay caused on your panel: a train that arrives late because you held it loses points, a train you keep moving earns them.

Setting a route

Routes are set signal to signal, entry to exit. Tap a round manual controlled signal to pick it as the entry. The panel then highlights only the valid exits for that entry: the next automatic signal downstream (a short route), the next manual controlled signal downstream, or — only at the panel edge or a terminus buffer stop — the boundary arrow or buffer stop. Tap a highlighted exit and the route is created between those two signals: the sections between entry and exit turn bold white when the entry signal is Clear (green), or take that signal's colour while it is at Approach, and where the exit is on the branch line the junction points swing to Reverse by themselves — loop and crossover points you work by hand, and no route ever knocks a hand-set point back — and the entry signal clears to Yellow, or to Green when the next signal is already Yellow or Green, only when the line ahead is free, there is no conflicting route, and no train is in the way. Hold Shift while you tap the exit to make the route persistent: a little white square appears beside the entry signal, and the route is NOT cancelled by the first train passing through it — it stays set until you click the entry again or right-click it. An ordinary route releases by itself behind the first train that passes through it, block by block. You never set points by hand for a normal route. Square signals marked AUTO are automatic block signals: they are never an entry signal, they clear and return to Red by themselves, and a long road is worked section by section — set a route at a manual signal till the next automatic, the automatic clears by itself, the train passes it, then set the next route at the next manual signal. No route carries across a manual signal on its own, and no manual signal is skipped. If a route cannot be set — the line is occupied, a conflicting route is already set, or points on the route have failed — the hint bar and the Log tell you the reason for that exact signal. Nothing fails silently.

Cancelling

Select the entry signal of a set route and press Cancel route, or simply right-click the entry signal to cancel its route straight away. If a train is approaching the route, the route stays approach-locked for 2 minutes (game time) before it releases, because the driver may not be able to stop. Never put a signal back to Red in front of a fast train unless you must: a train that passes a Red signal without authority is a SPAD and ends the shift. A signal turning Red behind a train that has already passed it is normal working, not a SPAD.

Reading the panel

Grey track: clear, no route. Bold white track: route set and its signal is Clear (green) — the route is made, from where to where, and that train may pass. A routed section whose signal is not yet Clear takes that signal's colour: yellow for Approach, flashing yellow for Approach Medium. Red track: occupied — and only ever the ONE section between two signals that the train is actually in (signals A, B, C make sections A–B and B–C; a train in A–B reddens A–B only, B–C does not change). No second train may enter a red section — its signal, controlled or automatic, stays Red in front of it — unless you deliberately order that train to Pass at danger. Flashing orange: track circuit failure, treat the section as occupied. Aspects: Red = Stop, Yellow = Approach, Flashing Yellow = Approach Medium, Green = Clear, and a small white light under a Red aspect is the call-on light. Points squares: white = normal, yellow = reverse, flashing purple = failed, leave it alone until it recovers. Trains run on the track line itself as small black headcode berths with a direction arrow — the border colour is the train type colour, and freight berths are drawn longer than passenger berths. Hover a berth (desktop) or tap it (mobile) for speed, next stop, delay, origin to destination, platform, type and length, plus the emergency tools. There are no other boxes on the board: one train, one berth. Signals are small dots on the line with a tiny short code (ALB1, SYR2) — the full signal code is in the Selected panel and in the signal's tooltip. Block sections carry no labels on the board: occupancy is the red track itself.

Call-on (permissive moves)

Call-on is OFF by default. To use it, pick the entry signal first, turn Call-on ON (the button in the entry panel under the board, or the C key while the entry is selected), and only then tap the exit: that is the one case where a route may be set into an occupied section, for example to join another train standing in a platform. The signal stays Red with the white call-on light lit, and the driver proceeds slowly on sight, ready to stop short of the train ahead. Without Call-on, a route into an occupied section is refused and the panel tells you why.

Driver calls

A train held at a Red signal phones the panel after about 2 minutes (game time): read the call in Comms, it names the signal it is standing at, and you can answer it with the Reply button on the call. A train still standing at a platform boarding passengers never phones about the road — it is dwelling, not waiting on you. You can also message the neighbouring box from Comms: ask them to hold a train, or warn them one is running late, and they answer in Comms. Pass at danger is the one deliberate exception to a Red signal: it is for a train held at a Red signal — controlled or automatic — that cannot clear because the section ahead is occupied, or because of a failure (a track circuit failure or failed points). No train ever enters an occupied section on its own; only this command sends one in, on purpose. Set the points by hand for that move (this is the one time you work points by hand), tap the train's berth, and choose Pass at danger: the driver then proceeds on sight, slowly, to the next signal. After any SPAD or failure, examine the line in the Log before you let the train continue.

Scoring

Arrive on time: +100. One to two minutes late: +50. Three to five minutes late: +20. Over five minutes late: +0. Penalties, always explained in the Log: a late arrival costs 10 points per minute beyond two minutes late, up to -50. A train that stops at the wrong platform costs -25. A train routed past a booked stop without stopping costs -50. Wrong route at a junction: -100. A clean shift with no wrong routes and no incidents earns a +200 bonus. Identifying a train showing ??? earns +50; letting it leave unidentified costs -30. Removing a train with the emergency tool costs -40. Stars follow your score and your average delay.

Progress and unlocking

The first map in each area is open. Every minute you actively signal a main map counts towards unlocking the next one and towards your rank, from Trainee up to Route Controller; finishing a map with at least 1 star unlocks the next one straight away. Good performance, on-time arrivals and clean shifts, is what earns those stars; poor performance, wrong routes and long delays, holds them back. Idle time, Practice, Watch and Community maps do not count. Each new area adds harder layouts, more traffic, and new failure types to deal with.

Controls

Drag to pan the panel, scroll or pinch to zoom, or use the + / - / Fit buttons under the board. Esc clears a selection and closes popups. C toggles call-on on the selected signal. Shift + tap the exit signal makes a persistent route. Right-click an entry signal to cancel its route, and right-click a train's berth (or its row in the Trains tab) for the train menu: Assign new service, Reset service, or Reverse train, each with a confirmation. Click a platform bar on the board to see that platform's own timetable, and click a train's berth for its full details — hovering never pops up info boxes on this panel. On small screens the panel tabs (Comms, Trains, Timetable, Log) open from the bar at the bottom of the screen.
